# Bowcaster (house-rules wizard kit)

Rifiyr's kit, as written by the owner (2026-10-12). The rules text is kept
as given; "How the app models it" at the end is ours.

## Bowcaster

Wizard Kit (no need to dual class anymore)

**Description:** Bowcasters are half-elves who have learned to harness the magical nature of their elven blood through the grounded, in-the-world nature of their human blood. The elven half gives them mystic insight into what is about to happen in the physical world — where a boar will break cover, where a man will plant his foot, where a spell will land. The human half gives them the instinct to act on that insight through something they can hold in their hands. That something is the arrow. A Bowcaster casts magic through material objects, and nothing suits him better than an arrow: intent given a point, a shaft, and a direction, then let go.

**Worldview — Shooter, Arrow, Target:** A Bowcaster sees every problem, in a fight or out of one, as three things. The target is the result — what actually has to happen. The arrow is the means — the one thing that has to be sent to make it happen. The shooter is the discipline to send it cleanly and spend nothing on anything else. This makes Bowcasters direct to a fault. They want the shortest line between themselves and a result, and they prize clarity above nearly everything — not because situations are simple, but because you can't shoot what you can't see. Nothing irritates a Bowcaster like someone deliberately muddying the water.

The worldview in practice:

*The boar.* A dire boar has been tearing up a farmstead's fields for a week, and tonight it comes for the barn. The farmer's sons want to meet it at the fence with spears. The Bowcaster tells them to stay behind the rails and hold the lantern high, then walks out to the lip of the drainage ditch and waits. He doesn't watch the boar. He watches the ditch. A charging boar hates uncertain footing, and he knows — not guesses, knows — that this one will veer left where the bank has crumbled. When it does, the arrow is already in the air, and it takes the beast through the foreleg joint mid-turn. It goes down thirty feet short of the fence. Target: the charge ends before the fence. Arrow: one shot to the foreleg. Shooter: knowing where the boar would turn before the boar did.

*The griffon.* A griffon has taken to the high pass and is picking off the mule trains one animal at a time. It never lands where an archer can reach it, and it never flies the same line twice. The Bowcaster spends a morning on a ledge doing nothing but watching it hunt. By noon he has stopped watching where it is and started watching the air in front of it — the eddy off the cliff face it uses to bank, the moment its wings open wide to catch the lift. The heavy shaft he looses goes to empty sky, and the griffon banks into it. It takes the arrow through the wing root at full spread and comes down on the scree, alive, furious, and grounded. Target: the griffon on the ground, not merely bleeding. Arrow: a heavy shaft to the wing root. Shooter: shooting at where the wing would be, not where it was.

*The wall of fire.* An enemy mage has thrown a wall of fire across the only ford, and a friendly column is stacked up on the far bank with hostile cavalry coming up behind. Dispel magic reaches 120 yards. The wall is a full two hundred yards out, and the enemy line is between. Any other wizard would have to walk into bowshot of that line to drop it. The Bowcaster nocks an arrow, casts dispel magic onto the shaft, and looses it at the ford — nearly twice the distance the spell could reach on its own. Where the arrow lands, the wall goes out, and the column crosses before the cavalry closes. Target: the ford open. Arrow: a spell carried past its own range. Shooter: the only caster on the field who could put the magic where it was needed without moving from where he stood. This is the shot only a Bowcaster can make — archery and magic sent down the same shaft.

**Requirements:** Bowcasters must have Wisdom and Intelligence scores of 15 or higher. They must be of good alignment — any good.

**Role:** Bowcasters exist to protect human, elven, and half-elven communities, and to promote understanding among the three — a half-elf belongs to all of them and fully to none, and a Bowcaster makes that his work. They are problem solvers, and often troubleshooters in the most literal sense: when a village has a problem an arrow can settle, a Bowcaster is who they send for. His training is built for exactly that — he can hunt and track the beast that's been taking the livestock, then observe and investigate the stranger who turns out to be behind it, all with the same patient attention. That makes him equally at home as a wilderness guide, a town's quiet eyes, or a dungeon's scout. In a fight, he doesn't waste arrows on whoever is closest. His role is to reach the target the enemy is most trying to protect — the caster in the back rank, the officer giving orders, the archer behind cover no one else can touch. The Bowcaster's way of joining the two bloods can't be taught to a pure elf or a pure human; it is a half-elven art, and Bowcasters keep it that way.

**Secondary Skills:** Required: Bowyer/Fletcher.

**Weapon Proficiencies:** Bowcasters may take proficiency in any type of bow — short bow, long bow, or any other bow variant your setting includes — with no restriction to a single size or type.

Beyond his bows, a Bowcaster may take proficiency in only one weapon from the standard Wizard weapon list — the discipline leaves no room to master more than that. A Bowcaster suffers an additional –2 penalty to hit with any weapon that is not a bow (stacking with the ordinary non-proficiency penalty if he isn't proficient in it), including that one other weapon — a Bowcaster's training sharpens the bow arm at the expense of everything else. In practice, melee is something a Bowcaster falls back on when he has no other choice, not a real second option.

**Attacks Per Round:** A Bowcaster fires at a bow's normal rate of fire — two shots per round for any bow he's proficient with, exactly as any other proficient archer would — but only when he's shooting ordinary arrows. In any round he casts a spell through an arrow, that Spellarrow is his only shot; he doesn't get a second, ordinary arrow on top of it (see Spellarrows, below).

**Nonweapon Proficiencies:**
Bonus: Hunting, Tracking, Observation, Investigation.
Recommended: Spellcraft, Fire-building, Herbalist, Reading/Writing, Blind-fighting, Running, Survival.

The four bonus proficiencies come free, but they come in place of something: a Bowcaster does not receive the nonweapon proficiency slots a 1st-level wizard would normally get from his class to spend as he likes. Between the discipline's demands and his own early study of magic, he had time to learn nothing else — hunting, tracking, observing, and investigating are what his training gave him, and that's all. Any extra slots granted by a high Intelligence score are still his to spend as he pleases, and he gains new slots as he advances in level like any other wizard. A Bowcaster with Tracking takes only half the usual non-ranger penalty: –3 to his Wisdom score for the check rather than the full –6. He makes a capable tracker, but not a ranger's equal.

**Literacy and Feather-Script:** Unlike most wizards, Bowcasters are not literate by default — Reading/Writing must be taken as a proficiency like anyone else's, which is why it appears in the recommended list. What they have instead is a script of their own: a form of braille cut into feathers. The edge of a feather is trimmed into a pattern a Bowcaster reads by running a fingertip along it, and he can leave messages for other Bowcasters this way — a feather tucked into a doorframe or tied to a branch means nothing to anyone else. This is also how a Bowcaster keeps his magic. Rather than a spellbook, he carries a collection of feathers, each trimmed to record a single spell. The collection serves as his spellbook for every purpose — memorizing, copying, replacing — and a lost or ruined feather is a lost spell, exactly as a torn-out page would be. A Bowcaster may learn a new spell from another caster who teaches it to him; he may learn one from a spellbook or scroll only if he can actually read it. Note the distinction with scrolls: a literate Bowcaster can study a scroll to learn its spell, but he can never cast a spell directly from one (see Special Hindrances).

**Equipment:** All Bowcasters start out with a finely-crafted, masterwork bow of their choice — its quality grants a +1 bonus to hit and a +1 bonus to material saving throws. They also start with a quiver of equally finely-crafted arrows, free of charge, carrying that same +1 bonus to material saving throws.

**Durability:** Bowcasters are also master bowyers and fletchers in their own right. Any bow or arrow one makes for himself is of finer quality than one produced by any other bowyer or fletcher, and carries that same +1 bonus to material saving throws. This covers every material saving throw the item is ever called on to make, but applies most especially to the crushing blow check to determine if an arrow breaks when shot. It applies only to his own craftsmanship, not to a bow or arrow he picked up somewhere else.

## Special Benefits

**Bonus Spells:** A Bowcaster begins play already knowing warp wood and Murdock's feathery flyer, entered in his feathers at no cost. These two spells don't count against his normal known-spell total for a 1st-level wizard — they come free, on top of whatever he'd otherwise know.

### 1. Mystic Archery

Mystic Archery is the name the discipline gives to everything a Bowcaster can do with a bow that an ordinary archer can't — and does without casting a spell. These talents are neither spells nor tricks of pure skill. They are what happens when a lifetime of archery is trained around the elven gift for sensing what comes next: some are always on, some he calls on deliberately, and all of them are innate — no spellcasting, no material components, nothing to prepare, and nothing another wizard could learn from a book. Between them they cover the whole of a Bowcaster's craft: reading a shot before it's made, keeping a stray arrow off a friend, finding the one seam in an enemy's armor, freezing a man in place with nothing but the threat of the string, and putting a shaft around a corner. That same gift is what closes the divination school to him (see Special Hindrances, below): a mind already tuned to what will happen can't be bent to the divinations other wizards learn.

**Mystic Aim (1st Level)** — The heart of the discipline. A Bowcaster's edge isn't a steadier hand; it's a form of divination — not into distant places or hidden thoughts, but into time. The elven blood gives him an intuitive feel for what's about to happen in the physical world, and the human blood grounds it in something he can hold: the arrow goes not to where the target is, but to where it's most likely to be. It's a strong sense, not a certainty — otherwise he'd never miss at all — which is why it shows up as a bonus to hit rather than a guarantee. That insight lets him add his Wisdom's Magical Defense Adjustment to his bonus to hit with a bow, on top of his normal Dexterity Missile Attack Adjustment. It's always active, on every shot — he doesn't need to spend a round aiming to benefit. Bow attacks otherwise use the standard Wizard THAC0.

**Unfriendly Fire (2nd Level)** — A special kind of called shot. Before rolling to determine who is actually struck when firing into a melee (PHB, Chapter 9, Firing into a Melee), a Bowcaster may take a penalty to his eventual attack roll — any amount he chooses — and add that same number to his intended target's weight in the random determination. He's trading accuracy for certainty — the harder he pushes to be sure who the arrow is headed for, the less likely he is to actually hit them once it gets there. This applies to any melee he fires into, allies present or not — it's just as useful for making sure a stray shot doesn't drift onto the wrong enemy.

**Direction Arrow (3rd Level, 1/day)** — By concentrating over an ordinary arrow for a full round and then loosing it straight up, a Bowcaster can make it hover and turn mid-air to point steadily toward a specific person, place, or object he has directly seen or touched before, provided it lies within 1 mile per caster level, holding that heading for up to 1 turn per caster level. Beyond that range the arrow simply falls. It reveals direction only, never distance or condition, and this is an innate talent rather than a spell — it doesn't run afoul of the ban on divination magic below.

**Anticipate Blow (4th Level, 1/round)** — Once per round, when any attack — melee or missile, it makes no difference — targets the Bowcaster, he may attempt to anticipate it: make a Wisdom check. If it succeeds, his AC improves by an amount equal to half his caster level (round down) against that one attack only. It doesn't negate or parry the attack outright — it can still hit if it's good enough even against the improved AC — and the bonus doesn't carry over to anything else that round.

**Impressive Shot (5th Level)** — The Bowcaster's called shots come easier than most. Any called-shot penalty he takes is reduced, and the reduction grows as he does: –1 at 5th level, –2 at 10th, –3 at 15th, and –4 at 20th. The same benefit applies to Unfriendly Fire (above): its target-weight bonus starts higher by that same amount, free of any to-hit cost, before the normal one-for-one trade kicks in.

**Stopping Aim (6th Level, 1/day)** — A Bowcaster may use this talent one of two ways, decided in the moment:

- *Intimidate:* Rather than firing, he may spend a full round drawing and holding his aim on a single target within range, as a pure intimidation display. At the end of the round, the Bowcaster makes a Wisdom check. If it succeeds, the target must succeed on a saving throw vs. paralysis or be frozen in place by dread of being shot, unable to act. The target repeats the save at the start of each of its rounds; the fear holds until it succeeds, or until the Bowcaster looks away or lets his aim drop.
- *Shoot the Charge:* If the Bowcaster's Initiative roll for the round beats that of an opponent who is moving to close with and attack him — or to close with and attack someone else he can see — he may declare Shoot the Charge and loose an arrow at that opponent immediately, resolved before the opponent's movement or attack takes effect. If the shot hits, the charge itself breaks: the target stops advancing for the rest of the round, wherever it's gotten to. Covering a companion this way works exactly like covering himself; the charger just has to be within his range and line of sight. He doesn't lose his normal action for the round — but it slips to the very end of the initiative order. Whatever else he meant to do that round, he does last, after everyone else has acted. Winning the initiative buys him the shot; the price is spending the rest of the round at the back of the line.

**True Shot (7th Level, once per enemy per combat)** — Before rolling to hit, a Bowcaster may attempt a True Shot against a given enemy: first make a Wisdom check. If it succeeds, treat that enemy's Armor Class as 2 points worse for the remainder of the combat, representing a seam in its armor or a gap in its shield he's now found and keeps exploiting — this reduces only the AC granted by worn armor and shields, nothing else. If the Wisdom check fails, the attack is resolved as a normal shot instead. Either way, once he's tried True Shot against a particular enemy in a fight, he can't try again against that same one until the fight ends — but nothing stops him from attempting it fresh against a different enemy.

**Bank Shot (8th Level)** — A Bowcaster can curl a shot around whatever stands between him and his target. He does it by warping the arrow's own shaft in flight — the same principle as warp wood, but innate, wordless, and done in the instant the arrow leaves the string. The shaft never survives it: a Bank Shot arrow is destroyed every time, whether the shot succeeds or fails, and no material saving throw applies. Before firing at a target behind cover, make a Wisdom check. If it succeeds, the shot ignores partial cover entirely — no AC bonus for the crate, the tree trunk, or the shield the target is crouched behind. If the check succeeds by more than 5 (a roll at least 6 below his Wisdom score), the shot ignores full cover as well: he can put an arrow into a target he can't see at all, provided he knows where it is and the arrow has a direct and realistic flight path to reach it — around a corner, over a wall, through an open window, but never through a keyhole. Ignoring cover is all it does; any other obstacle, range penalty, or modifier still applies as normal. If the check fails, the shot is resolved normally, cover and all.

**Aimed Shot (9th Level)** — If a Bowcaster spends a full round doing nothing but sighting his target — no movement, no other action, no spellcasting — two things happen beginning the following round. He gains a +2 bonus to hit with his bow, starting with his first shot at that target, and he moves to the top of the initiative order, acting first. Both persist from round to round for as long as he holds his ground and keeps shooting: plant his feet, and the +2 and the first action stay with him. If he moves, the +2 is lost — but he still acts first for the rest of the combat. Only another full round of aiming will bring the +2 back.

### 2. Spellarrows (Arrow-Delivered Casting)

Bowcasters can cast certain spells onto their arrows and fire those arrows normally, allowing the spell to affect targets or areas beyond their own reach. Casting and firing happen in the same round as a single combined action: for initiative purposes, add the spell's casting time to the bow's weapon speed factor, and resolve the shot at that combined segment. The spell-carrying arrow is then fired as a normal ranged attack. What a missed attack roll means depends on the spell's tier — see below. A Bowcaster can fire only one such arrow per round, and in a round he does so it is his only shot — casting through an arrow forfeits the bow's second shot from rate of fire, so a Spellarrow is never a free attack tacked onto a normal volley. This is also the Bowcaster's native way of casting: a spell sent through an arrow is cast at full speed and full strength, while one cast the ordinary way is slower and weaker (see Special Hindrances, below).

*Note:* This combined-initiative rule applies only to spells cast live in the same round they're fired. A pre-fletched Spellfletching arrow (see below) already carries its spell — firing one is a normal ranged attack at the bow's ordinary weapon speed, with no casting time added.

*Note:* Firing a Spellarrow is still a shot with a bow. It combines freely with everything in Mystic Archery — Mystic Aim's to-hit bonuses apply to it, it can be the shot that follows a round of Aimed Shot, and it can be delivered as a True Shot, a Bank Shot, or the shot from Stopping Aim's Shoot the Charge if the situation calls for it, exactly as an ordinary arrow would.

This ability improves in three tiers as the Bowcaster gains levels:

**1st Level — Touch Spells.** The Bowcaster may cast any touch-delivered spell onto an arrow. The spell takes effect only if the arrow makes contact with its target (see Delivery against armor, below). All saving throws apply as usual. Whether the arrow also wounds is a separate question, settled by the same roll. Spells that can only be cast upon the caster's own person (e.g., change self, chill touch) are never eligible.

*Delivery against armor:* A touch spell doesn't need to penetrate armor to work — the arrow only has to make contact. For the purpose of delivering the spell, ignore any AC the target gains from worn armor and shields (bracers of defense included); Dexterity, and magical protection that isn't armor-shaped — a ring of protection, displacement, blur — still count. Roll the attack once and read it two ways: if it beats this delivery AC, the spell takes effect; if it also beats the target's true AC, the arrow deals its normal damage as well. A shot that clears only the delivery AC touches and discharges the spell but glances off the armor without wounding. A fighter in plate mail with no Dexterity bonus, for example, is AC 10 for delivery even though he's AC 3 to the arrow itself. A natural 1 is still a misfire (see below).

*Errant touch-arrows:* If the arrow misses the intended target but a viable alternate target for the spell is present, the DM may rule the spell affects that target instead, using the grenade-like missile scatter rules (DMG, pp. 62–63) to determine where the errant shot falls.

**5th Level — Point-of-Origin Area Spells.** The Bowcaster may cast onto an arrow any spell whose area of effect is centered on a point the caster chooses (e.g., fireball, ice storm, stinking cloud, web, cloudkill). The arrow's landing point becomes that point of origin. If the spell normally allows the caster to choose the size and shape of the area (such as wall of fire's curtain-or-ring option), the Bowcaster retains that choice as normal — he decides the shape when the spell is cast, and the arrow's landing point simply becomes the origin point from which that chosen shape is oriented, exactly as if he had cast the spell standing there himself.

The Bowcaster must have line of sight to the intended landing point, as with any normal called shot. Because these spells target a point rather than a creature, a missed attack roll does not waste the spell — the arrow simply lands somewhere other than the intended point, and the area of effect (in whatever shape was chosen) is centered there instead. (A natural 1 is handled separately; see Misfires, below.)

**9th Level — Self-Origin Area Spells.** The Bowcaster may cast onto an arrow any spell whose area of effect is normally embodied within or emanates from the caster's own person (e.g., burning hands, cone of cold, fear, shout, globe of invulnerability). The arrow's landing point becomes the new point of origin, as though the Bowcaster himself stood there. For cone- or line-shaped effects, the area continues outward along the arrow's flight path from its point of impact.

As with point-of-origin spells above, a missed attack roll does not waste the spell — the effect simply originates wherever the arrow actually lands rather than where it was aimed.

### 3. Spellfletching (Replaces Scroll Scribing)

In place of arcane scroll scribing, a Bowcaster may inscribe a spell he knows onto a specially prepared arrow shaft and fletching. This "spell arrow" holds the spell until fired, functioning as a single-use magic item.

- **Requirements:** The Bowcaster must know the spell and be of sufficient level to cast it normally. He may only fletch spells of a category he can currently deliver live via Spellarrows — touch spells at 1st level, point-of-origin area spells at 5th, and self-origin area spells at 9th.
- **Time:** 1 day per level of the spell (as scroll scribing).
- **Chance of Success:** 80%, +1% per Bowcaster level, –1% per spell level — the same formula used for ordinary scroll-writing. If the roll fails, the spell fizzles and the ink, quill, and material component are all consumed and wasted — the gold spent on the component is lost, and fresh materials must be gathered before trying again. An especially bad roll (natural 96 or higher) doesn't just fail outright — it produces a cursed spell arrow instead, indistinguishable from a proper one until it's fired.
- **Labeling:** Each spell arrow must be marked with a personal sigil, notch pattern, or fletching dye so the Bowcaster can distinguish it from mundane ammunition on sight. An unmarked or mismarked spell arrow risks being loosed by accident in an ordinary volley — treat as a misfire (see below) if this occurs.
- **Use:** Fired as a normal ranged attack, but only one spellfletched arrow may be shot per round — the other shot from rate of fire, if he takes it, must be an ordinary arrow. Delivery follows the same rules as a live-cast Spellarrow of the appropriate tier (touch, point-of-origin, or self-origin), including the caster's choice of shape for spells like wall of fire.

**Special Cases:** Three spells fletch into something other than an ordinary single-use spell arrow. A Bowcaster may create and own a combined total of these magic arrows — arrows of accuracy, seeking arrows, and arrows of flame together — equal to his caster level. To make another once he's at his limit, one of the existing ones must be spent, lost, or allowed to expire.

- **Arrow of Accuracy (1st level):** The 1st-level spell accuracy fletched onto an arrow — same time, cost, and chance of success as any 1st-level spell arrow — produces a +1 arrow of accuracy: +1 to hit and +1 to damage, and the bonus is magical — it counts as a +1 weapon against creatures that can only be struck by magic — lasting 1 day per caster level from the moment it's finished. It can be fired, recovered, and fired again for as long as the enchantment holds, subject to the usual breakage checks; when the duration runs out it's an ordinary arrow again.
- **Seeking Arrow:** The spell seeking fletched onto an arrow holds far longer than the spell normally would — 24 hours from the moment it's finished, rather than the spell's usual 10 turns. The arrow's target is chosen when it's loosed, not when it's fletched: the Bowcaster names any target he can see as he fires, and the arrow seeks that target and nothing else, curving around obstacles as the spell allows but never passing through a solid barrier. Once fired it's spent, whether it found its mark or not.
- **Arrow of Flame (3rd level):** The 3rd-level spell flame arrow is the true exception to a spell arrow being single-use. Fletched onto an arrow through the usual process — same time, same cost, same chance of success as any 3rd-level spell arrow — it produces a permanent +1 arrow of flame: +1 to hit, and +1 fire damage on top of the arrow's normal damage, every time it's fired. It's a real magical arrow and can be recovered and reused like any other, subject to the usual breakage checks (which his Durability bonus helps with, if he made the shaft himself). Each casting yields exactly one such arrow.

**Materials and Component Rarity**

A spell arrow requires two distinct kinds of material: the arrow itself must be finely made — fancy feathers for the fletching, and fancy wood, metal, stone, or bone for the shaft and arrowhead — and it must then be inscribed with the spell's runes using fine ink and a quill. Neither half is ordinary craft-shop work. It's the ink's ingredient specifically that scales in rarity with the spell's level:

| Spell level | Component rarity |
|---|---|
| 1st | Uncommon |
| 2nd–4th | Rare |
| 5th–8th | Exotic |
| 9th | Unique |

The DM has final say over what the component actually is — a griffon talon, moondew gathered at the equinox, the shed skin of a will-o'-wisp, ink brewed from a wyvern's venom. The player may suggest something fitting for their spell, but the DM decides. When choosing, weigh the rarity tier against how much of that creature or substance genuinely exists in the campaign world: requiring a phoenix feather for an "uncommon" 1st-level component in a setting where phoenixes are the stuff of legend isn't a fair uncommon requirement — it's a unique one in disguise, and needlessly cruel to a player who has no way to know that going in. Match the fictional rarity to how findable the thing actually is at your table, not just to the label on this chart.

The quill has no such tie to a specific creature — it simply needs to be fine and unused, clearly a cut above an ordinary goose feather (a peacock plume, a swan feather, one dipped in silver, or similar). Each quill is good for exactly one spell arrow.

**Cost**

The first time a Bowcaster successfully fletches a given spell, roll (2d6+3) × 10 gp, then double that result for every spell level above 1st — the cost doubles at each step up the ladder, same as the component's rarity does. This result becomes the spell's permanent fletching cost from then on — the going rate, now that a source has been identified — though the component must still be reacquired and paid for every time a new arrow is fletched with that spell; it's a recurring cost, not a one-time purchase.

| Spell level | Cost | Average |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | 50–150 gp | 100 gp |
| 2nd | 100–300 gp | 200 gp |
| 3rd | 200–600 gp | 400 gp |
| 4th | 400–1,200 gp | 800 gp |
| 5th | 800–2,400 gp | 1,600 gp |
| 6th | 1,600–4,800 gp | 3,200 gp |
| 7th | 3,200–9,600 gp | 6,400 gp |
| 8th | 6,400–19,200 gp | 12,800 gp |
| 9th | 12,800–38,400 gp | 25,600 gp |

Nothing here entitles a player to simply hand over gold at a shop and walk out with a finished spell arrow — the cost only applies once a supplier or source for the component exists. Uncommon and some rare components might reasonably be bought outright; exotic and unique ones typically can't be, and finding a source in the first place is its own piece of adventure.

**Misfires (Natural 1)**

A natural 1 on the attack roll is worse than an ordinary miss. The arrow goes badly astray, and the DM determines where it lands and, for cone- or line-shaped effects, what direction it faces on impact, taking into account terrain, the Bowcaster's position, and anything else at hand.

- For a touch-delivered spell, determine where the errant arrow actually goes the usual way (the grenade-like scatter rules, or the random target among those nearby). If it strikes a viable target for the spell, the spell takes effect on that target, saving throws as normal. Only if it strikes nothing viable is the spell lost.
- For a point-of-origin or self-origin spell, an ordinary miss still lands the arrow somewhere nearby and the spell goes off there (see above). A natural 1 instead leaves the outcome entirely to the DM's judgment — the arrow could go wide enough to land well off the mark, catch unintended bystanders, or, if it ends up near the Bowcaster or a cone/line effect faces back toward him, catch the Bowcaster himself. There is no fixed formula; the danger is real because the outcome isn't scripted.

## Special Hindrances

- A spell cast onto an arrow is bound to the arrow. The Bowcaster's magic goes into the shaft, not the air, and once it's loosed there is no calling it back — the spell lives or dies with the shot. A missed touch-arrow loses the spell outright, and an errant area-arrow goes off wherever the arrow actually lands (see Spellarrows and Misfires, above).
- Casting the ordinary way comes harder to a Bowcaster, because he never really learned it. His whole training is in sending magic down a shaft; shaping a spell in empty air, the way other wizards do from their first day, is a skill he practiced only enough to get by. Without an arrow to carry it, the spell fights him: any spell not cast through an arrow has its casting time increased by 3, and deals 1 less damage per die it would normally inflict (to a minimum of 1 per die). Spells sent through a bow — cast live as a Spellarrow or pre-fletched — suffer neither penalty.
- Bowcasters may not cast spells from the school of divination. The innate foresight of Mystic Archery already occupies the part of the mind those spells would use; a Bowcaster who tries to learn one finds it simply won't take.
- Bowcasters may never wear any armor. It isn't only the usual wizard's trouble with iron and gesture — armor deadens the senses Mystic Archery depends on. A Bowcaster reads the world through skin and ear and the tension of the string, and anything that comes between him and the moment dulls his foresight.
- Bowcasters suffer a –2 penalty to hit with any weapon that is not a bow. His training is the bow and nothing else; the hours another wizard spends learning to hold a dagger properly, he spent on the string.
- Bowcasters cannot cast a spell from a scroll, and cannot scribe scrolls — scroll scribing is replaced by Spellfletching (see above). A scroll asks its reader to release magic from parchment straight into the air, which is exactly the kind of casting a Bowcaster never learned; the words are legible to a literate Bowcaster, but inert in his hands. Scrolls are not useless to him, though: a Bowcaster who can read may study a scroll to learn the spell on it, copying it to a feather in the usual way (see Literacy and Feather-Script). He can learn from a scroll; he simply can't fire one off.
- A Bowcaster's training in magic is narrow by design. Everything he knows about casting, he learned through the bow; the broader schooling in the handling of magic that other wizards take for granted was never part of it, and it shows in what he can't do:
  - He cannot use wands, rods, or staves restricted to wizard use. The knack of activating one — the trained reflex most wizards pick up alongside their first cantrips — was simply never taught to him. Staves carry one narrow exception: a Bowcaster who knows warp wood can cast it on a wizard's staff to bend and reshape it into a bow of whatever size and shape he chooses, without disturbing the staff's own magic. Warp Staff is the only way he will ever make use of one, and it's potent enough that Bowcasters have a reputation for it. Ask any archmage: a Bowcaster can scarcely look at a fine staff without his fingers itching. "This could have been a perfectly good bow!" is the complaint attributed to Corin Ashfeather, a Bowcaster remembered for saying exactly that about a staff of the magi, moments before its owner stopped him mid-cast.
  - He cannot brew potions or salves. He never received the training, and his art has no place for it.
  - His creation of magical items is limited to bows and arrows. He may enchant a bow, craft magical arrows, and fletch spell arrows (see Spellfletching), but wondrous items, cloaks, rings, and anything else outside archery are beyond him. His craft stays where his focus is.

## Table rulings

- **Spellarrow eligibility is strict (2026-10-12):** a spell rides an arrow
  only if it fits a tier he has reached (touch at 1st, point-of-origin area at
  5th, self-origin area at 9th). Anything else, such as Melf's Acid Arrow, is
  cast the ordinary way, with the casting penalty.
- **Bracers of defense aren't armor (2026-10-12):** a Bowcaster can wear them
  (PHB: bracers aren't armor). This is about his own AC; when he delivers a
  touch Spellarrow, the kit still ignores the *target's* bracers for the
  delivery roll, as written.

## How the app models it

Numbers the tools work out (THAC0, damage, initiative speed) follow the
kit; everything else is announced at the table, like other characters'
abilities (PLAN decision 23). Rifiyr's save: `samples/rifiyr_10-12.json`,
built by `scripts/convert-rifiyr.mjs`.

| Rule | In the app |
|---|---|
| Who gets the kit's rules | The class's settings (gear beside Primary/Secondary Class) → Custom Kit Rules → **Bowcaster**. Older saves without the setting go by the class name |
| Bow attacks use wizard THAC0 | Missile scenarios in the wizard group |
| Masterwork bow +1 to hit | On the bow's weapon row (it belongs to the bow) |
| Mystic Aim (Wis magical defense adj. to hit with a bow) | Hit Mod on each bow scenario (Rifiyr: Wis 15, +1) |
| Two shots per round with a bow | Bow scenarios at 2/1 |

An **Ammunition** list under Weapons holds his arrows (flight 1d6, sheaf 1d8, and any magic arrows with their own hit and damage adjustment). A bow attack picks its **Ammo**: the ammo sets the dice, and the bow's and the ammo's adjustments add up. Rifiyr has a flight and a sheaf attack for each bow |
| –2 to hit with anything but a bow | Hit Mod on each non-bow scenario, with the wizard non-proficiency –5 when not proficient |
| Casting without an arrow: casting time +3, –1 damage per die | Worked out automatically for a Bowcaster's Spell scenarios (sheet and DM Helper) |

| No armor, no scrolls, no divination, no wands/rods/staves | Table rulings; not enforced by the sheet |
